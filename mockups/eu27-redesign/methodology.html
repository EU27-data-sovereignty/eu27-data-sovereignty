1What this is, and what it is not

i

2How sources were found

3What must hold for a fact to be printed

Researched claims. The cited page was downloaded, its SHA-256 recorded, and the quoted text found by literal matching. Every number and date in the printed value appears in the original-language quote.

Eurostat figures. Read from a pinned dataset through its API and compared with the table cell, within 0.5%.

Categorical findings. Admitted only when a second, independent automated reviewer reached the same value from the same quote.

A value no checked source supports is withheld and shown as a gap. A gap means not yet sourced, never that the thing does not exist.

5Priority of a holding

Priority = consequence of loss (0–3) + difficulty of rebuilding (0–2) + exposure to non-EU providers (0–2)

Critical is 6 or more; High is 4 or 5. Infrastructure exposure counts only holdings whose source says where they run. Silence counts as not stated, never as national.

6Data-sovereignty placement

An input without a checked source counts as not demonstrated, never as sovereign and never as dependent. Each state is placed by the first rule it meets:

Dependent on non-EU providers. A tier 0 or 1 holding on non-EU infrastructure, or an eID or trust anchor outside state or EU control.
Sovereign in law and in practice. A statute, plus at least 75% of verified tier 0/1 holdings on national or EU infrastructure, state-controlled trust anchor and eID, and its own data centres or cloud.
Sovereign in practice, not secured in law. The practice test, without the statute.
Secured in law, not yet in practice. The statute, without the practice test.
Not demonstrated. Neither.
